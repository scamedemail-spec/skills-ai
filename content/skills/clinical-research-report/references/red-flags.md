# Symptom red flags

The general rule in this skill is that symptoms get researched, not triaged. This file is the exception, and it is deliberately short. Every item here is a presentation where delay carries irreversible cost and where a long literature review is the wrong response.

If any of these appears in the user's reported symptoms, **say so in chat immediately, before starting research**, in plain language: this presentation is one where same-day or emergency evaluation is the standard recommendation, and a research report is not a substitute for it. Then continue building the report.

A normal lab value does not clear a red flag. Troponin drawn early in an infarct is often normal; a normal D-dimer does not exclude PE in a high-probability patient; normal inflammatory markers do not exclude a surgical abdomen. Do not use a reassuring number from the panel to talk down a red-flag symptom.

## Cardiovascular
- Chest pain or pressure, particularly exertional, radiating to arm/jaw/back, or with nausea, sweating, or shortness of breath
- Sudden severe tearing chest or back pain
- Syncope, especially exertional or without prodrome
- New resting shortness of breath, orthopnea, or paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnea
- Unilateral leg swelling with calf pain, or pleuritic chest pain with breathlessness

## Neurological
- Any focal deficit: unilateral weakness, facial droop, new speech difficulty, sudden vision loss, new asymmetric numbness
- Sudden severe headache, particularly described as worst-ever or maximal at onset
- New seizure
- Altered consciousness, confusion, or new significant personality change
- Headache with fever and neck stiffness
- Progressive weakness, saddle anesthesia, or new bowel/bladder incontinence with back pain

## Respiratory and infectious
- Hemoptysis
- Fever with rigors, or fever with hypotension, confusion, or rapid breathing
- Fever in someone immunosuppressed, on chemotherapy, asplenic, or neutropenic
- Difficulty breathing or stridor

## Abdominal and GI
- Severe or rapidly worsening abdominal pain
- Rigid abdomen or pain with guarding
- Hematemesis, coffee-ground emesis, melena, or significant hematochezia
- Jaundice of new onset, particularly with pain, fever, or confusion
- Persistent vomiting with inability to keep fluids down

## Systemic and oncologic
- Unintentional significant weight loss
- Drenching night sweats with fever or lymphadenopathy
- New unexplained bruising or bleeding, or petechiae
- Severe or rapidly worsening pain of unclear cause

## Obstetric
- Any of: vaginal bleeding, severe abdominal pain, severe headache with visual changes, or reduced fetal movement in a known or possible pregnancy

## Psychiatric
- Suicidal ideation, self-harm intent, or intent to harm others

For this one, do not proceed as a research task at all. Respond directly and supportively as a person, stay with the user, and surface crisis support (in the US, 988 Suicide and Crisis Lifeline, call or text; internationally, findahelpline.com). Do not deliver a lab report as the response to a disclosure of suicidal intent, and do not use the end-conversation tool in these cases.

## Toxic and metabolic
- Suspected overdose or poisoning
- Known or suspected anaphylaxis: facial or throat swelling, hives with breathing difficulty
- Symptomatic hypoglycemia with confusion or inability to self-treat

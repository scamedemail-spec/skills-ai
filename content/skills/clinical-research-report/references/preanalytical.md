# Pre-analytical factors

A substantial share of "abnormal" lab results are explained by how, when, or under what conditions the sample was collected, not by disease. Check this before building any differential. For several analytes a pre-analytical explanation belongs at or near the top of the ranked list.

## Ask the user

1. Fasting status, and how long
2. Time of day of the draw
3. Strenuous exercise in the preceding 24-72 hours
4. Acute illness, infection, or injury in the preceding weeks
5. **Biotin supplementation** (including hair/skin/nail products and B-complex)
6. All supplements, not just medications
7. Alcohol in the days before the draw
8. For menstruating users, cycle phase, for hormone panels
9. Recent significant change in weight, diet, or training load
10. Known difficult draw, or a note of hemolysis on the report

## What each factor distorts

**Fasting status** — glucose, triglycerides most strongly; insulin; to a lesser degree total and LDL cholesterol. Non-fasting lipids are acceptable under current guidance but triglycerides and calculated LDL are affected, which matters when comparing panels drawn under different conditions.

**Time of day** — cortisol (large diurnal swing), testosterone (higher in morning; a low afternoon result is often not interpretable), iron and TIBC, ACTH, growth hormone. TSH has a modest diurnal pattern that can matter at the margins.

**Recent strenuous exercise** — CK can rise dramatically and persist for days; AST and LDH rise with it; troponin can rise after endurance events; creatinine rises transiently; WBC rises acutely. An unexplained CK/AST elevation in someone who lifts or runs hard has an obvious first-line explanation.

**Acute illness or inflammation** — ferritin, CRP, ESR, fibrinogen, and platelets are acute-phase reactants and rise with any inflammation. Albumin, transferrin, and often iron fall. Ferritin in particular is routinely misread as iron status when it is actually reflecting inflammation, so pair it with CRP and transferrin saturation before drawing an iron conclusion.

**Biotin** — interferes with a wide range of biotin-streptavidin immunoassays. Effects vary by assay design and can push results either direction. Commonly affected: TSH, free T4, free T3, troponin, PTH, vitamin D, ferritin, and reproductive hormones. High-dose biotin producing a result pattern resembling Graves disease is a well-documented artifact. Ask about it explicitly; users frequently do not consider a hair supplement to be a medication.

**Hemolysis** — falsely elevates potassium, LDH, AST, and magnesium; can lower sodium. A markedly high potassium with no clinical context and no supporting ECG story is a hemolyzed sample until proven otherwise. Look for a hemolysis note on the report.

**Lipemia and icterus** — interfere with multiple photometric assays in either direction depending on method.

**Tourniquet time and posture** — prolonged tourniquet raises potassium, calcium, protein, and albumin through hemoconcentration. Standing versus supine draw shifts protein-bound analytes by a several-percent margin.

**Hydration state** — dehydration concentrates hemoglobin, hematocrit, albumin, sodium, and BUN. Overhydration dilutes them. A hemoglobin or sodium that moved across two draws may simply reflect fluid status.

**Alcohol** — GGT most sensitively; AST and the AST:ALT ratio; triglycerides; MCV with sustained use; uric acid.

**Menstrual cycle phase** — required context for estradiol, progesterone, LH, FSH. A hormone panel without a cycle day is largely uninterpretable in a menstruating person.

**Supplements with direct analyte effects** — vitamin D and calcium supplementation, iron supplementation raising ferritin and transferrin saturation, B12 supplementation raising serum B12 while functional deficiency may persist (methylmalonic acid is the better marker), creatine supplementation raising serum creatinine and lowering calculated eGFR without any change in actual renal function.

**Medications that shift specific analytes** — a non-exhaustive prompt to check the user's list: statins and liver enzymes, metformin and B12, PPIs and B12 and magnesium, diuretics and electrolytes and uric acid, corticosteroids and glucose and WBC, SSRIs and sodium, lithium and TSH and calcium, amiodarone and thyroid function, hormonal contraceptives and thyroid binding proteins, total T4, SHBG, and triglycerides.

## How to use this

For every analyte in the queue, ask before ranking: is there a pre-analytical explanation on this list? If yes, it goes into the differential at a rank reflecting how likely it is given what the user reported, and it is named explicitly in the report. If the user did not answer the intake questions, note the specific unresolved factor rather than a generic caveat, e.g. "fasting status unknown, which limits interpretation of the triglyceride and calculated LDL values."
